# Handloom Heritage of Bagalkote: GI Ilkal Sarees, Guledgudd Khana, and Kasuti Embroidery

## 1. Ilkal Saree (Geographical Indication Tagged)
The **Ilkal Saree** is the quintessential textile identity of Bagalkote district, woven in the ancient town of Ilkal and surrounding rural settlements.

### Distinctive Technical Features:
- **Tope Teni Seragu (Pallu)**: The hallmark of an authentic Ilkal saree is its signature red/maroon silk pallu featuring three solid red bands separated by two white temple-spire bands (*Tope Teni*).
- **The Kondi Technique**: The cotton warp of the saree body is joined to the silk warp of the pallu using a series of interlocked loops known as the *Kondi* jointing technique. This ensures seamless strength and a distinctive structural ridge.
- **Traditional Borders**:
  - *Chikki Paras*: Subtle dotted border pattern.
  - *Gomi Border*: Chevron or arrow-head pattern.
  - *Gayatri Border*: Broader geometric multi-ribbed pattern.
- **GI Registration**: Granted in 2006 under the Geographical Indications of Goods Act (GI Application No. 43).

## 2. Guledgudd Khana (Geographical Indication Tagged)
**Guledgudd Khana** is India’s first and only handwoven blouse fabric to receive a dedicated Geographical Indication tag (GI Application No. 373, awarded 2014).

### Characteristics:
- **Purpose**: Crafted in the hill town of Guledagudda to perfectly complement Ilkal sarees.
- **Motifs**: Woven on pit and frame looms with specialized *dobby* attachments. Features raised, lustrous geometric motifs with deep cultural meanings:
  - *Choukadi*: Diamond check pattern.
  - *Tulasi Katti*: Sacred basil altar motif.
  - *Siddeshwara Pheta*: Ceremonial turban motif.
  - *Anne Kalu*: Elephant footprint pattern.

## 3. Kasuti Needlework
- A traditional, intricate counted-thread embroidery indigenous to the region.
- Practiced by rural women artisans who embroider geometric motifs on Ilkal sarees and Khana cloth without drawing outlines or using knots.
- **Four Classical Stitches**:
  - *Gavanti*: Double running line stitch.
  - *Murgi*: Zigzag step stitch.
  - *Negi*: Running weaving stitch.
  - *Menthi*: Cross stitch.

## Inclusive Growth & Handloom Tourism
The Bagalkote District Administration promotes **Handloom Tourism**, organizing guided trails to weaver mohallas, direct cooperative showroom sales, and artisan workshops to eliminate exploitative middlemen and ensure living wages for traditional weaving families.
