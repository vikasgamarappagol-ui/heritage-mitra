# Mahakuta and Banashankari: Sacred Springs, Living Temples, and Rural Fairs

## Mahakuta: The Valley of Sacred Springs
Mahakuta is a serene Shaivite pilgrimage site located 14 km from Badami, tucked away in a lush, wooded valley shaded by ancient banyan trees and sandstone cliffs.

### The Temple Cluster & Sacred Pools
- **Mahakuteshwara Temple**: Built in the late 6th to early 7th century CE in Early Chalukyan Dravidian style. It features stone lattice windows with floral arabesques and ornate pilasters.
- **Vishnu Pushkarini (Lotus Pool)**: A natural freshwater spring pool fed continuously by perennial underground natural currents. In the center of the pool rests a submerged five-faced Shiva Linga (*Panchamukha Linga*). Devotees and pilgrims take ritual cleansing baths in its clear waters.
- **Historical Inscription**: The famous **Mahakuta Pillar Inscription** of King Mangalesha (dated 602 CE) recorded the king's military victories and religious endowments of ten villages to the temple.

## Banashankari Temple (Cholachagudda)
Located 5 km from Badami in the ancient forest known as *Tilakaaranya*, the Banashankari Temple is one of the most revered Shakta pilgrimage destinations in North Karnataka.

### Devotion to Goddess Shakambhari
- Dedicated to Goddess Banashankari (an aspect of Parvati), known as *Vanadurga* ("Goddess of the Forest") or *Shakambhari* ("She who provides vegetables and nourished beings during famine").
- The presiding stone deity depicts the eight-armed Goddess seated upon a roaring lion, trampling the demon Danava underfoot while holding divine weapons.

### Haridra Tirtha & Architectural Features
- **Haridra Tirtha (Harischandra Tirtha)**: A large square stepped tank surrounded on all four sides by colonnaded stone mandapas.
- **Deepastambha**: A towering three-storied stone lamp tower and watchtower rising from the stepped waters of the tank.

### The Banashankari Jatre (Annual Rural Fair)
- Held annually during the holy month of **Pushya (January-February)** around the full moon (Banada Hunnime).
- Spans nearly a month and constitutes one of the largest rural cultural and economic events in Karnataka:
  - **Religious Core**: Grand Rathotsava (chariot procession) and sacred tank bathing.
  - **Rural Commerce**: Thousands of temporary trade stalls selling GI Ilkal sarees, Guledgudd Khana, bell-metal utensils, agricultural tools, and local delicacies.
  - **Folk Entertainment**: Traditional rural drama troupes (*Company Nataka*), Sannata, Bayalata, and wrestling matches.
