# Aihole: The Cradle of Indian Temple Architecture and Epigraphical Center

## The Experimental Laboratory of Indian Builders
Aihole, historically known as **Ayyavole** or **Aryapura**, is universally revered as the **"Cradle of Indian Rock and Temple Architecture"**. Between the 4th and 12th centuries CE, early Chalukyan master craftsmen, guilds, and sculptors gathered here to experiment with diverse structural engineering forms, floor plans, plinth profiles, and spire configurations. With over **125 stone temples** spread across village fields, hillocks, and riverbanks, Aihole preserves the transitional evolution from rock-cut caves and wooden mandapas to mature structural stone temples.

## Headquarters of the 'Ayyavole 500' Merchant Guild
In medieval times, Aihole was the international headquarters of the famous **Ayyavole 500** (*Ainnurvar*), a powerful transnational trading guild whose commercial networks stretched across the Indian subcontinent, Sri Lanka, Southeast Asia (Sumatra, Java), and China. The guild financed temple construction, maintained private armies, and patronized scholars and artists.

## Key Architectural Highlights

### 1. The Durga Temple Complex (Late 7th - Early 8th Century CE)
- **Unique Apsidal Plan**: Built in an apsidal (*Gajaprashtha* - elephant back) floor plan with a rounded sanctum and an open, colonnaded peristyle passageway that wraps around the entire monument.
- **Iconographic Masterpieces**: Contains some of the finest sculpted relief panels in Indian art:
  - Dynamic **Mahishasuramardini** driving her trident through the buffalo demon.
  - Majestic **Varaha** gently lifting the earth goddess Bhudevi.
  - Harihara, Ardhanarishwara, and Shiva with Nandi.
  - Ornate celestial couples (Mithunas) carved on the veranda pillars.

### 2. Lad Khan Temple (c. 5th - 6th Century CE)
- One of the oldest surviving structural stone temples in the Deccan.
- Modeled on ancient wooden assembly halls, featuring a rectangular hall with massive square pillars, perforated stone lattice (*jali*) screens admitting soft natural light, and a stepped flat roof surmounted by a small upper shrine.

### 3. Meguti Jain Temple & The 634 CE Ravikirti Inscription
- Situated on the summit of Meguti Hill, built by the royal court poet **Ravikirti** in **634 CE (Saka 556)** during the reign of Emperor **Pulakeshin II**.
- **Epigraphical Importance**:
  - Contains a 19-line Sanskrit inscription composed in classical Champu metre.
  - Records the genealogy of the Early Chalukyas and Pulakeshin II's military campaigns, including his historic victory over Emperor Harsha on the banks of the Narmada River.
  - Explicitly references the Mahabharata war and establishes the historical antiquity of classical Sanskrit poets **Kalidasa** and **Bharavi**.

### 4. Ravana Phadi Cave Temple (c. 6th Century CE)
- A rock-cut Shaivite cave shrine carved into sandstone rock.
- Features a life-sized dancing Shiva Nataraja flanked by the Sapta Matrikas (Seven Mothers), Bhringi, and Ganesha, sculpted in deep relief.

### 5. ASI Archaeological Museum Aihole
- Located inside the Durga Temple enclosure. Houses prehistoric stone tools, rare Chalukyan bronzes, hero stones (*Veeragallu*), inscriptions, and sculpted masterpieces gathered from surrounding ruins.
