# Pattadakal: UNESCO World Heritage Monuments and Architectural Synthesis

## Historical & Sacred Significance
Pattadakal, historically known as **Raktapura** or **Pattada Kisuvolal** ("Red Ruby City of the Crown"), was the sacred ceremonial site where the coronations (**Pattabhisheka**) of the Early Chalukyan kings were solemnized. Situated on the left bank of the sacred northward-flowing (uttaravahini) **Malaprabha River**, Pattadakal was inscribed as a **UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987** under criteria (iii) and (iv) as an exceptional testimony to an eclectic architectural art that achieved a harmonious synthesis of northern and southern Indian building traditions.

## The Architectural Synthesis: Nagara and Dravida
Pattadakal is world-famous among art historians because it stands as a living open-air laboratory where two distinct Indian architectural styles stand side-by-side:
1. **Dravida-Vimana (Southern Style)**: Multi-tiered pyramid superstructures, miniature kutas and salas, and square crowned shikharas.
2. **Rekha-Nagara-Prasada (Northern Style)**: Curvilinear towers with miniature gavaksha arches crowned by an Amalaka (ribbed stone disc) and Kalasha.

## Major Monuments in the UNESCO Complex

### 1. Virupaksha Temple (Lokeshwara Temple - c. 740 CE)
- **History**: Built by Queen Lokamahadevi to celebrate the military triumph of her husband, King Vikramaditya II, over the Pallavas of Kanchipuram.
- **Significance**: The largest and most complex temple at Pattadakal. Directly modeled on the Kailasanatha Temple of Kanchipuram, and served as the direct architectural inspiration for the world-famous monolithic rock-cut **Kailasa Temple at Ellora**.
- **Architecture**: A mature Dravidian vimana with an ornate Nandi Mandapa housing a colossal polished black stone Nandi.
- **Sculptures & Epigraphs**: Relief panels depicting episodes from the Ramayana, Mahabharata, and Bhagavata Purana. Inscriptions commemorate the master builder **Gundan Anivaritachari**, bestowing upon him the title *Tribhuvanachari* ("Master of the Three Worlds").

### 2. Mallikarjuna Temple (Trailokyeshwara Temple - c. 745 CE)
- **History**: Built concurrently with the Virupaksha Temple by Queen Trailokyamahadevi (sister of Lokamahadevi).
- **Architecture**: Features a four-tiered Dravidian vimana crowned with a circular griva and dome-shaped shikhara.
- **Art**: Exquisite narrative friezes of Krishna subduing the serpent Kaliya, the lifting of Govardhana Hill, and lively animal tales from the Panchatantra.

### 3. Sangameshwara Temple (Vijayeshwara Temple - c. 720 CE)
- **History**: Initiated by King Vijayaditya (696–733 CE).
- **Significance**: One of the oldest surviving structural stone temples at Pattadakal in pure Dravidian style, characterized by monumental simplicity, massive square pillars, and high plinth moldings.

### 4. Galaganatha Temple (c. 750 CE)
- **Architecture**: Exemplary Northern **Rekha-Nagara** style featuring a fully preserved curvilinear spire with delicate gavaksha latticework.
- **Key Sculpture**: A dynamic eight-armed Shiva slaying the demon Andhaka (*Andhakasuravadha*).

### 5. Papanatha Temple (c. 740 CE)
- **Architecture**: A transitional hybrid combining a northern Nagara shikhara with southern Dravidian decorative wall niches and pillared vestibules. Features 25 elaborate narrative relief panels recounting the entire epic of Rama.

### 6. Jain Narayana Temple (9th Century CE)
- Located 1 km west of the main cluster on the Badami road. Built during the Rashtrakuta period (reign of King Krishna II / Amoghavarsha I) in refined Dravidian style with two large stone elephants flanking the porch.

## Cultural Celebrations
- **Pattadakallu Dance Festival**: Organized annually in January by the Department of Tourism and Department of Kannada and Culture, featuring premier classical dance exponents against illuminated monuments.
- **Virupaksha Rathotsava**: Annual chariot festival celebrated in March-April by local communities.
