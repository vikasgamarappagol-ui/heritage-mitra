Bagalkote District Tourism

Official District Portal: Bagalkote, Karnataka, India

Major Tourism Destinations

  • Badami: Famous rock-cut cave temples dating back to the Chalukya dynasty (6th-8th century). Key sites: Cave 1 (Nataraja), Cave 2 (Vishnu/Trivikrama), Cave 3 (Vishnu/Narasimha/Varaha), Cave 4 (Jain Tirthankaras), Agastya Lake, Bhoothanatha Temple, Badami Fort, Archaeological Museum.
  • Pattadakal: UNESCO World Heritage Site on the banks of Malaprabha River. Renowned for its fusion of Nagara and Dravidian temple architecture. Key temples: Virupaksha, Mallikarjuna, Sangameshwara, Galaganatha, Papanatha.
  • Aihole: Cradle of Indian rock architecture. Over 125 temples spanning 4th-12th centuries. Highlights: Durga Temple (apsidal plan), Lad Khan Temple, Meguti Jain Temple with Ravikirti inscription (634 CE), Ravana Phadi cave temple.
  • Mahakuta: Group of 6th-7th century Shaivite temples dedicated to Mahakuteshwara, natural spring-fed Pushkarini lotus tank, and Shaiva monastery.
  • Banashankari: Sacred temple dedicated to Banashankari Devi (Shakambhari) in Tilakaaranya forest, Haridra Tirtha kalyani, annual Banashankari Jatre.
  • Kudala Sangama: Confluence of Krishna and Malaprabha rivers, Aikya Mantapa of social reformer Jagadjyothi Basaveshwara, Sangameshwara Temple.
  • Almatti Dam: Lal Bahadur Shastri Sagar reservoir on Krishna River, Mughal and Rock Gardens, musical dancing fountains, boating facilities.
  • Ilkal: Famed weaving cluster producing GI-tagged Ilkal Sarees known for Tope Teni seragu (pallu) and Kasuti embroidery.
  • Guledagudda: Traditional handloom center famous for GI-tagged Guledgudd Khana (choli/blouse fabric).
  • Mudhol: Historic princely state, home to the Mudhol Hound canine breed, and Mudhol Fort.
  • Jamkhandi: Historic princely state palace, Ramateerth, and Pampa Sarovara.
  • Bilagi: Historic monuments, Siddheshwara Temple, and ancient stepwells (baolis/bowlis).

Handloom Tourism Initiatives

Bagalkote District Administration actively promotes handloom tourism to connect visitors directly with weaver clusters in Ilkal and Guledagudda, providing authentic insight into GI-tagged handloom craftsmanship.
